## A. Code review against the plan

Plan alignment first. Implemented as planned: criteria list, class weights 0.60/0.25/0.15 with renormalisation, coverage factor `1 - 0.15 x unknown weight`, one best variant per model, MMR off when a make or model is named, template explanations tagged spec/expert/owner, relaxation with named violations, vague and unsupported-brand handling, LLM parser with quoted-evidence grounding, cache, cap and stats. Not in the plan but implemented: ASSUMED_BOOST, a 2x penalty for known-missing required items, twin-pair limit growth, weight overrides, a `drop` argument.

### Findings

**A1. HIGH. No price preference when no budget is stated.** `criteria.py:206-220` (`_value`). Without a budget the only value field is features-per-lakh (a percentile). Cheap cars carry few features, so they score low. `weights.py:50` ("budget" profile boosts value_for_budget 2.5x) and `weights.py:47` (student) therefore boost nothing price-related. The plan (section 5) says "price against the stated budget and against same-segment cars"; the second half is not implemented. Confirmed: `recommend("cheapest car")` top 8 starts with XUV 3XO Rs 11.9 lakh, Nexon Rs 14 lakh; Dzire Rs 9.4 lakh is 6th. Held-out h-015 ("cheapest automatic ... can't go above 8 lakh") returns Rs 10-17 lakh cars. Fix: add a segment-relative inverse price percentile (lower is better) to `_value` when no budget exists, and a smaller one when a budget exists.

**A2. HIGH. No budget means the most loaded variant represents each model.** `diversify.py:28-33` picks the best-scoring variant. Comfort and tech are absolute booleans that saturate at 1.0 for top trims, and nothing penalises price. Confirmed on "SUV but not diesel": Duster Iconic Rs 17 lakh, Seltos HTX(A) Rs 18.8 lakh, Hector Rs 19 lakh. Brezza, Elevate, Taigun rank 27, 28, 23 of 31. Same root cause as A1; fix A1 first, then consider choosing the representative variant nearest the segment-median price when no budget is given.

**A3. HIGH. A missing crash rating silently leaves the safety criterion.** `criteria.py:113-121` and `_weighted_mean` (`criteria.py:88-106`). Criterion value is the mean over known fields, so an unrated car is scored on airbags, ESC, ABS, TPMS only. Confirmed on the catalogue: mean safety 0.864 for 5-star, 0.708 for 4-star, 0.767 for unrated (469 of 832 rows). The same partial-field problem applies to every criterion (a car with unknown sunroof, cruise control and HUD is averaged over what is left). The coverage factor (`score.py:115-123`) only counts criteria that are wholly unknown, so partial gaps cost nothing. This breaks the plan's principle ("a car with gaps is ranked slightly lower"). Fix: weight each criterion's unknown fields into `unknown_w` (field weight share x criterion weight), or use a neutral prior (segment mean) for the missing star field plus the unknown flag.

**A4. HIGH (parser, not recsys). A stated upper bound is inverted, and unparsed text is silent.** `services/intent_v2.py:348` (`min_pre` contains "above" with no negation check). Confirmed: "I can't go above 8 lakh" gives `budget.min = 800000`, `max = None`, and v3 answers with Rs 10-17 lakh cars and no message. "500 litres se zyada boot space" is not parsed (only `se\s*zyada` after a price pattern, `intent_v2.py:349`) and the answer is just sedans. `api.py` never shows the user the unexplained residual unless the LLM is on. Plan principle 1 ("a stated must-have is never broken silently") fails here. Fix: negation-aware bound words ("can't/cannot/won't go above, not more than" = max), and return a `not_understood` list from the residual (numbers with units, "lakh", "km") so the UI can say it.

**A5. MEDIUM. Percentiles are computed on the filtered candidates, not on the segment, and over variants.** `api.py:285` passes `cands` to `score_candidates`; `score.py:90` calls `compute_criteria(cands, ...)`; `criteria.py:228-230` forms the segment from candidate rows. Confirmed: the same row (variant 32, XUV 3XO petrol manual) scores running_cost 0.479 under "suv" (522 candidates) and 0.335 under "suv under 12 lakh" (171 candidates). Under a tight filter "the best of a few" looks like "the best". A model with 30 variants outweighs a model with 2 in every percentile. Tiny groups (< 5) fall back to all candidates, which is a different population again. Fix: compute percentiles once on the full catalogue (one row per model-fuel-transmission, or variant weighted 1/n_variants), cache them, and use them for any filter.

**A6. MEDIUM. MMR decides membership, not only order.** `diversify.py:54-72, 90-106`. Plan section 7 says re-ranking. Final display is sorted by score (`diversify.py:106`), so the stated display rule holds. But the 8 shown models differ from the top 8 by score in 62 of 189 eval queries (confirmed by script); never in the top 3. Shortlist cases lost to MMR: Scorpio-N (plain-02, score rank 8), Innova Crysta (hing-03, rank 8), XUV 7XO (budget-19, rank 7). Cause: `similarity` (`diversify.py:36-45`) gives 0.3 for "same body type", which is constant when the query fixes body type, so make and price dominate. Scorpio-N (Rs 20 lakh, Mahindra SUV) vs Thar Roxx (Rs 20 lakh, Mahindra SUV) = similarity 1.0. Fix: drop the body term when all candidates share a body type (renormalise), and consider lambda 0.85.

**A7. MEDIUM. Thin expert/owner evidence gets full class weight.** `score.py:109-113`. A model with 2 of 13 aspects gets its whole 0.25 from those 2. Confirmed by planting two 1-star aspects on Nexon: it fell out of the top 8 of "suv under 15 lakh"; every other car's coverage fell from 1.0 to 0.59-0.71 even though no data changed for them. Fix: shrink the class sub-score toward the segment mean by n_known/n_total (or require a minimum aspect count). Redistribution with empty files is correct (confirmed: overall = spec sub, coverage 1.0, `evidence.empty` True), but `api.py:309` still reports `classes` as 0.60/0.25/0.15, which is not what was applied. Report the effective class weights.

**A8. MEDIUM. "Like X" is a filter only.** `filter_v2.py:133-141`; `api.py` passes nothing about similarity to scoring. Confirmed on named-04 "something like Creta": the owner's Kushaq/Taigun/Elevate rank 17, 19, 23 of 27 while the smaller XUV 3XO and Venue rank high. Fix: add a similarity term (price ratio, length, body) to the score when `reference_models` is set.

**A9. MEDIUM. Segment-flat features and ties make the lower half uninformative.** `criteria.py:118`: airbags use a percentile; when most cars have 6 airbags, a 6-airbag car scores about 0.5. `criteria.py:182-187` performance uses a fixed gearbox ordinal (AMT 0.2) with no source; that is a hand-set table, against the plan's "no hand-set point values" for scales. Low-medium; fix by treating airbags as an absolute ladder like stars.

**A10. LOW-MEDIUM. Explanation claims.** `explain.py:146` says "Crash rating: not tested yet"; the data only says "not on record" (`api.py:31` uses that wording). `explain.py:84` says "ahead of about N% of similar {fuel} cars here" even when the fallback population (all candidates of that fuel, any body) was used (`criteria.py:73-85`). `explain.py:94` "good feature count for that money" is shown for any value >= 0.5 (the median). Fixes: change the wording; say "among N candidates" with the population actually used.

**A11. LOW. `relax.py:274-275`: `like_model` violation is added unconditionally** to every car when that move is relaxed, even a car that sits inside the band. Not confirmed by a run (no eval case relaxes it). Fix: test the band.

**A12. LOW. `api.py:72-117` `_drop_table` lacks exclude_makes, exclude_features, max_* and budget parts, while `relax.py` can relax them.** `apply_drops` ignores an unknown key without saying so (`api.py:124-129`). Users can see "relaxed" labels they cannot switch off.

**A13. LOW. Weight chips and new labels are not reachable.** `new_car_service.py:334-336` calls `recommend(user_query, drop=drop, app_root_path=...)` only, so `weight_overrides` is never passed. `static/script.js:56` still computes "Great match" from `ranking_score / topScore`, ignoring `match_label`. Plan step 4 is open.

**A14. LOW. Dead code, duplicated constants, performance.**
- `CriteriaResult.detail` (`criteria.py:63-66, 92-104, 242-247`) builds a per-row, per-field dict for every candidate and no caller reads it (grep finds no use). It is most of the 0.7 s spent on a 700-row unfiltered query (measured: median 0.12 s, max 0.74 s over 209 queries).
- `config.MAX_NEAREST_VARIANTS` is unused.
- `KMPL_UNTRUSTED_FUELS` exists in `ranking_adapter.py:26`, `score.py:32` (`filter_v2.py` imports the first); `UNRATED_SAFETY_LABEL` in `api.py:31` and `filter_v2.py:72`; `COMFORT_FEATURES`/`TECH_FEATURES` in `weights.py:69-70` duplicate `criteria.py:49-53`.
- `api.py:291` `variant_counts[car["model"]]` is keyed by model only. Safe today (no model name is shared across makes; checked), fragile later.

**A15. LOW. `ASSUMED_BOOST` is applied after scoring** (`api.py:287-289`): `scores.overall` on the card includes it, `spec` does not, and the match label sees the boosted value. Small, but the sub-scores no longer add up to the overall.

### Checked and found sound

- Filter unknown handling: blank mileage (251 non-CNG rows), blank boot space (43) are kept and labelled when `unverified_fields` lists them; none of 25 scored columns is both valued and listed as unverified. Seats, price, transmission_type have no blanks in this catalogue (so the NaN-comparison exclusion in `filter_v2.py:120-149` is latent, not live).
- Hard constraints are not dropped by v3 itself; the only silent drops are parse misses (A4). Relaxed results carry named violations: 15/15 and 12/12 truthful, 0 mislabelled.
- Direction: the only lower-is-better quantity (price vs budget) uses `budget_fit` correctly (over budget loses 2.0 per 100%). No division by zero (`criteria.py:105` guarded by `den > 0`, price > 0 guarded, `pct_rank` empty group returns 0.5, `mmr_select` guards `hi > lo`).
- Determinism: sorts break ties on variant_id; `to_jsonable` sorts sets; relaxation combos sort on cost, size, keys, labels. No dict/set ordering dependence found.
- LLM parser: closed vocabulary, quoted evidence must be a substring of the query, numbers must derive from the evidence, rules win on conflict, errors fall back to rules (`llm_parse.py:444-535, 703-769`). Default is off (`llm_parse.py:185-186`), the plan says on (R1): a deliberate difference, noted.

## D. Shortlist analysis (40 queries, 191 owner cars)

Method: ran the v3 pipeline step by step (parse, filter, relax, score, best variant per model, MMR) for each query and compared with the engine output in `review_*_main.json`. Names were matched on "Make Model" exactly as the runner does, and also with a fuzzy catalogue key.

**Totals.** v3 shows 145 of 191 owner cars in its top 8; current shows 140. v3 misses 46:

| Cause | Count |
|---|---|
| 1. Model not in the catalogue | 0 (all 191 owner cars exist under the exact name used; 12 makes, 62 models in the catalogue) |
| 2. Removed by a hard filter | 12 (3 queries: named-01, named-02, named-07) |
| 3. Passes filters, ranked below 8 or diversified out | 34 (31 ranked 9th or lower; 3 were in the top 8 by score but dropped by MMR: Scorpio-N, Innova Crysta, XUV 7XO) |
| 4. Name matching in the runner | 0 |

Cause 2 detail: for "Hyundai Creta", "Scorpio N diesel automatic" and "Innova Crysta" the parser sets `models=[that model]`, and the filter correctly returns only that model. The owner expects the named car plus its rivals (Seltos, Taigun, XUV 7XO, Fortuner, Hycross, Ertiga...). The filter follows the plan ("Hyundai Creta -> only Creta"), so this is a spec question for the owner, not a bug. Both engines behave the same way.

Cause 3 is where the real signal is. 15 of 34 come from three queries with no budget and a loose constraint: hing-29 (5 misses), neg-02 (5), neg-08 (5). Score gaps are usually small near the cut (8th car 0.665 vs Nexon 0.652 and Brezza 0.649 in plain-09).

**Five most instructive cases**
1. **neg-02 "SUV but not diesel" (0/5 for v3, current 1/5).** No budget, so A1/A2 apply: v3 shows Duster Iconic Rs 17 lakh, Seltos Rs 18.8 lakh, Hector Rs 19 lakh. Owner cars Elevate, Brezza, Taigun, Kushaq rank 18-28 of 31, Grand Vitara 11th (0.751 vs cut 0.767). Current puts Grand Vitara second.
2. **plain-09 "manual petrol SUV under 12 lakh".** Nexon 9th (0.6516) and Brezza 10th (0.6492); the 8th car is 0.665. A gap of 0.013-0.016 decides it; these are the cars where small tuning moves the result. Brezza is 2nd in current (it ranks cheap, efficient, ADAS-free cars up; v3 does not).
3. **plain-02 "diesel SUV under 20 lakh".** Scorpio-N is 8th by score (0.707) but MMR shows XUV 3XO (10th, 0.693) instead, because Scorpio-N is same make, same body, same price as Thar Roxx (similarity 1.0). A6.
4. **hing-34 "wireless charging, 20 lakh tak".** Nexon 11th (0.784), Syros 13th, Grand Vitara 14th, all within 0.012-0.037 of the 8th (0.796); current has Nexon 1st, Grand Vitara 3rd, Syros 7th. The v3 top is Verna, Duster, Seltos, Hector: bigger, dearer cars again (A1/A2).
5. **named-04 "something like Creta" (2/5; current 0/5).** Kushaq 17th, Taigun 19th, Elevate 23rd of 27. The candidates pass the size and price band, but no similarity enters the score (A8), so smaller feature-rich cars (XUV 3XO, Venue) lead.

**Where the current engine beats v3** (3 queries: hing-29, neg-02, hing-34; v3 beats it in 6: plain-01, budget-05, named-04, named-06, named-11, num-12; 31 tie). All three losses share the pattern: no budget or a loose one, and owner cars that are mainstream Maruti or value picks (Swift 1st, Brezza, Grand Vitara 2nd/3rd). Current ranks on price and mileage. v3 ranks on feature richness and has no price pull (A1, A2). v3's wins come from the queries where a budget is stated or the set is large and its relevance to a spec is better (plain-01 hatchback: v3 gets Baleno and WagonR, which the current engine misses).

## Design changes 2026-11-15

Three owner-approved design changes, one commit each, nothing else touched. No LLM calls (`--llm off`). Constants are all in `services/recsys/config.py`.

| Change | Commit | Files |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Unknown crash rating gets a neutral prior | 9882576 | `criteria.py`, tests |
| 2. Price criterion, cheap/value intent, displayed variant | dd290a0 | `intent_v2.py`, `criteria.py`, `weights.py`, `score.py`, `diversify.py`, `explain.py`, `api.py`, `config.py`, tests |
| 3. Thin evidence counts for less | 1a12059 | `evidence.py`, `score.py`, `api.py`, `config.py`, tests, `tests/data/golden_empty_evidence.json` |

**1. Crash-rating prior.** When a car has no crash rating, its star field takes the median of the known crash-rating values in its segment (same `Reference` populations as the percentiles, one weight per model; segments with fewer than `MIN_SEGMENT_MODELS` models use the whole catalogue) as its value inside the safety criterion. The field still counts as unknown for the coverage factor (unknown fraction 0.3 of the safety weight), the car is still flagged `unrated_safety` and listed as "Crash rating: not available", and the criterion detail carries the prior as `prior_score`, never as `score`. Only the crash rating gets a prior. A prior alone never makes the safety criterion known.
Four otherwise identical cars, safety-weighted query ("family", "safety"), median 4: 5-star 0.7510, 4-star 0.7190, **unrated 0.7053**, 1-star 0.6231. Ordering 5 > 4 > unrated > 1 holds in two further segments (median 4 and median 3, tests). With median 5 the unrated car sits above the 4-star one but strictly below a 5-star car (the property tested: unrated < car rated at the median, equal before the charge). **In the real catalogue every rated model is 4 or 5 stars, so every segment median is 5**: an unrated real car is scored as a 5-star car minus the coverage charge, so it still beats a real 4-star twin. That follows from the rule as written; flagged below.

**2. Price criterion and displayed variant.** New spec criterion `price` = 1 - percentile of the variant's price in its segment (lower is better). Weight is a share of the spec class (the other spec weights share the remainder): `PRICE_DEFAULT_WEIGHT` with no budget, `PRICE_CHEAP_WEIGHT` with cheap/value wording (also when a budget is given), 0 with a budget and no cheap wording (the plan already scores price against the budget in `value_for_budget`, so that case is unchanged). Cheap/value wording is one regex, `CHEAP_INTENT_PATTERN` in `services/intent_v2.py` (cheap, cheapest, budget car / budget friendly, low cost, affordable, value for money, paisa vasool, sasta/sasti/sabse sasti, kam daam, kifayati, and close variants; a bare "budget 12 lakh" or "my budget is" is not cheap wording). It sets `intent["cheap_intent"]` and is also the "budget" use case. The explanation says "Price prioritised, as you asked" and leads the reasons. Displayed variant per model: most requested features known present, then lowest price (`displayed_variant_per_model` in `diversify.py`); score is that variant's own score; cards carry `missing_features`.
Two interpretations: (a) when the query is cheap and names no body type, the price percentile is taken over the whole catalogue rather than the segment, because a segment percentile cannot rank "cheapest car" by absolute price (the cheapest SUV and the cheapest hatchback would both score about 1.0); (b) when the ML assumed a body or fuel (a soft boost, never a filter), a variant matching the assumed one is preferred before price.

**3. Thin evidence.** Per car, the expert/owner class weight (after the old redistribution over the classes the car has) is multiplied by `expert_scale` = (relevant aspects covered / relevant aspects) x n/(n+`EXPERT_SOURCE_K`=1), n = distinct sources behind the covered aspects (relevant aspects = the expert aspects the query's profile boosts, all when none), and `owner_scale` = count/(count+`OWNER_SAMPLE_K`=50), count = largest per-theme sum of `posts_sampled`, `OWNER_NO_COUNT_SCALE`=0.5 without a count. The freed weight returns to the spec class; `class_weights` (new table column and card field) always sums to 1. No evidence in a class: unchanged. Empty files give results identical to the pre-change code on 8 queries (golden file generated from the code before this change).

### What was built
1. **Blank prior (`criteria.py`, config `BLANK_PRIOR_*`).** Criterion value = sum of weight x (known value, or the prior) / total applicable weight. Prior per field, from the same reference populations as the percentiles: percentile fields and the gearbox ordinal = segment median of the known values minus 0.15 (clipped to [0, 1]; for percentile fields this is about 0.35); yes/no features = segment share of True among the known cells (one weight per model) x 0.7. `BLANK_PRIOR_MODE = "flat"` gives 0.35 everywhere. A criterion whose fields are all blank is scored at the prior but stays NaN in `values`, so `unknown_criteria`, the unknown share (coverage factor) and the "not available" explanations are unchanged; `CriteriaResult.scoring` carries the scoring value. Priors appear in the detail only as `prior_score`, never as `score`. The crash-star prior is unchanged. Fields with no prior (price, budget fit) still drop out when unknown.
2. **Unverified tiers (`diversify.py`, `explain.py`, `api.py`).** Tier = `n_soft_flags` from `filter_v2` (unknown-kept required or excluded features, numeric minimums and maximums, unrated safety under a star minimum). Per model the variant with the fewest unverified requirements is shown (then best score); selection (MMR) and display go tier by tier, score order inside a tier. Cards carry `not_verified` ("ground clearance not verified", "sunroof absence not verified", "crash rating not verified"); the unknowns line for a minimum now names the field instead of the raw key. Mirrors `ranking_adapter.py:200`, no code imported.
3. **Review application (`evidence.py`, `score.py`, `weights.py`, config).** (a) `expert_scale` counts only aspects with a row for any model. (b) A model with no row in a class takes the mean sub-score and mean class scale of the reviewed models of its body-type segment (all models if the segment has fewer than 8); card score for that class is None, explanations say "Expert ratings / Owner ratings: none for this model yet", coverage unchanged. (c) Aspect weight = mean query weight (spec class) of the spec criteria it maps to (`EXPERT_ASPECT_CRITERIA`, `OWNER_THEME_CRITERIA` in config: my mapping, for the owner to review), 0 = not counted; a query with no preference (nothing but a budget) uses the overall rows only (expert overall, owner satisfaction). The old profile multipliers on expert_/owner_ criteria now drive only explanation order. (d) Aspects fed by one source label count once, at the mean of their weights. (e) Owner ratings keep the count-based value shrinkage; `owner_scale` is 1.0 for ratings (themes keep the count scale).

### Scoring
Per theme row: signal = direction (+1 / 0 / -1) x strength, strength = min(1, share / 0.25) x posts / (posts + 50) (`OWNER_SATURATION_SHARE`, `OWNER_SAMPLE_K`). A row counts with the query weight of its theme (`OWNER_THEME_CRITERIA`, the table the owner ratings already use; weight 0 = not counted; a query with no preference counts only `satisfaction`). Adjustment = `THEME_ADJUST_CAP` (0.25 of the sub-score range) x weighted mean signal of the rows the model has, added to the owner sub-score and clipped to [0, 1]. A model without themes gets exactly 0. Empty themes file reproduces the previous output (test, json equal).

Theme to criterion (config `OWNER_THEME_CRITERIA`, nothing new): reliability -> running_cost, value_for_budget; recurring_fault -> value_for_budget; build_issues -> value_for_budget, safety; service_cost -> running_cost, value_for_budget; service_experience -> value_for_budget; real_world_mileage -> running_cost; resale -> value_for_budget, price; satisfaction -> every spec criterion (the overall). There is no reliability criterion in the spec class, so reliability and faults go to the nearest ones. Themes only act when the owner class is active (owner ratings present); a model with themes but no ratings is scored at the neutral owner sub-score plus the adjustment.

### Explanations
- `expert_notes` (up to 2): stored Autocar notes. Candidates are labels whose engine aspects carry query weight; they rank by the largest profile multiplier of their aspects (comfort, mileage, family use case raise it), then mean weight, then score. Text: `Autocar India, <label> <score>/<scale> (<Mon YYYY>): <note>`; the note is the stored text, unmodified. `quote` is a separate object `{text, kind: "quotation", source, source_url}`. A score reason for the same label is dropped from `reasons` (the line carries the score).
- `owner_notes` (up to 2): themes relevant to the query, ranked by profile multiplier then strength; the strongest recurring fault always takes a place. Wording from the stored counts: share below 25% "Some owners", 25% to 50% "Many owners", over 50% "Most owners"; mixed: "Owners have mixed views on ..."; fault: "Reported fault: <label> (N of M reviews, CarWale)". Note appended.
- `caveats` (up to 3): every negative theme and reported fault the model has, relevant or not, `kind` = `reported_fault` or `negative_theme`, with source and `source_url(s)`.
- Every line has `source`, `source_url` (first sample URL for owners, review page for Autocar), and its text is also appended to the card's `match_reasons`. A model without rows gets empty lists and the existing "none for this model yet" wording. api.py has no compare output; none added.
