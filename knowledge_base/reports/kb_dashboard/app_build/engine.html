The recommendation engine

Turns a plain-language request into a ranked list of cars, with reasons. It never breaks a stated requirement silently, and it treats a missing value as unknown, not as no.

The scoring model in plain words

Weighted criteria, three evidence classes

Each car gets a value from 0 to 1 on criteria such as value for budget, safety, running cost, comfort, space, performance and tech. These are combined within three classes, and the classes are combined with fixed weights.

Specifications 60%Expert scores 25%Owner ratings 15%

Source: class weights and constants read from the engine configuration

Unknown stays unknown

A blank value is never shown as a fact. Inside the score it is valued below the typical car of its segment: for a yes/no feature, the share of segment cars that have it times 0.7. On top of that the overall score is multiplied by 1 − 0.15 × (share of weight that is unknown), so a car with gaps ranks slightly lower, not last.

Illustration for a segment in which half the cars have the feature. A known “no” is still worse than a blank. With 40% of the weight unknown the factor is 0.940.

Segment-relative scoring

Price, power and similar numbers are scored as a percentile among cars of the same body type, so a hatchback is not compared with a 7-seater on raw numbers. A segment with fewer than 8 models is compared with the whole catalogue.

Thin evidence counts for less

An owner rating is pulled toward the segment mean by count / (count + 50). A model with 959 ratings keeps 95% of its own value; one with 21 ratings keeps 30%.

What a result card shows

Every line on a card comes from a value the engine used, tagged with its evidence class and source.

1234567
  1. Variant and price. The variant shown is the best-scoring one for this query, with the lowest price in the model alongside.
  2. Requirement status. “Meets all your requirements”, or the requirement that was relaxed.
  3. Three bars. Specifications fit the search; expert and owner bars show standing among rated models of the same body type, not absolute quality.
  4. Why this car. Reasons built from the values used in scoring.
  5. What reviewers say. Stored note, score, source and date, with the quoted line and a link to the review.
  6. What owners say. Theme, direction and how many of the sampled reviews raised it.
  7. Caveats. Every negative theme and reported fault, shown whether or not the query asked about it.

Known limits

Taken from the review notes and recomputed from the result files where a number is shown.

  • The shortlist test is small and dated. 40 queries, 191 picks, labelled against the older catalogue. The 30 models added later were never on a label, so a new model that ranks first can count as a miss.evaluation results; shortlist gap review
  • The unknown-value probe is not met. When a feature is blanked on a good car, a car known to lack it ranks above it 4 times out of 75 probes (the target is 0).evaluation results; review of 15 Nov
  • Some impossible requests return cars. 4 of 15 requests labelled impossible still returned cars with no relaxation label in both engines. The review does not explain them.evaluation results
  • Parser misses remain. On the held-out set 2 queries returned a car that breaks a stated requirement (h-030, h-035); both engines share these parser misses. Unknown values never count as violations in this test.evaluation results
  • Review coverage is partial. 14 of 92 catalogue models have no expert rows and 12 models have owner ratings under 30 reviews. They get no review reasons and are not penalised.review data files
  • Expert scores are compressed. Overall expert scores run from 6 to 9 out of 10, so the engine compares cars; it does not rescale them.review data files
  • Newer models are scored on less. The review notes say the models added most recently carry no dimension, ABS, ESC or ADAS values yet, so they are scored on more unknowns until the knowledge base is filled.review of 15 Nov
  • Owner themes are a sample. Theme counts are mentions in a sample of reviews per model, not in all reviews, and a theme’s effect on the score is capped by design.review of 15 Nov
  • The language-model query reader is off by default. The design plan wanted it on; it is optional, needs a key, and falls back to the rules.review of 14 Nov; API notes

API endpoints

The same engine is available as a small typed HTTP service beside the web app.

  • GET/health
    status, engine version, catalogue row and model counts, data file dates.
  • POST/v1/recommend
    {query (1-500 chars), limit (1-20, default 8), llm (default false)}. Returns {engine, query_understood, results, relaxed, generated_at} plus message, data_as_of and llm.
  • GET/v1/models/{model}/evidence
    A catalogue model with no reviews returns empty lists; an unknown name returns 404.
  • POST/v1/compare
    {models: [2-4 catalogue model names], query: optional}. The engine has no compare function, so for each model the service parses the query once with the existing parser, pins the intent to that model and calls recommend(intent=...); you get that model's best-matching variant card side by side.
